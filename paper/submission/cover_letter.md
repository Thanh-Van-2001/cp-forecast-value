Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Does a more accurate load forecast catch more coincident peaks? A pre-specified evaluation in four US electricity markets", for publication in Applied Energy as a research article.

In ERCOT, PJM, NYISO and ISO-NE, large customers pay transmission or capacity charges set by their load in a few system peak hours. They curtail on days that a load forecast flags as likely peaks, and these forecasts are compared and sold on accuracy. The manuscript tests whether accuracy predicts how many coincident peaks a forecast catches. The test uses public EIA-930 data and the operators' own day-ahead forecasts for 2018 to 2026, 94 peak hours in all, and an analysis plan fixed in version control.

Across forecasts of very different quality, the more accurate ones caught more peaks. Among forecasts as good as the operators' own, accuracy said little. At equal error, the form of the error mattered: with the same number of alerts, errors that preserve the order of days caught 97 to 100 percent of peaks and noisy errors 79 to 80 percent. Forecasts that compress peaks failed when alert thresholds were taken from actual past peaks. The practical message for customers, forecast vendors and market operators is to compare coincident-peak forecasts by backtested capture at equal alert budgets, not by accuracy.

The manuscript reports two pre-specified hypotheses that were not supported and discloses four amendments to the analysis plan, two of them made after the results. Code, data-download scripts, the plan history and the results of every run are available at https://github.com/Thanh-Van-2001/cp-forecast-value.

The author also has a manuscript under consideration at Applied Energy (APEN-D-26-28856) on a different decision problem: battery scheduling in buildings, using different data. The two manuscripts share no data, analyses or text beyond general methodological framing.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. The use of generative AI tools is disclosed in the manuscript as the journal's policy requires. The author declares no competing interests and received no specific funding.
